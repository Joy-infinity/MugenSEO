# What actually moves rankings in 2026

Google uses hundreds of signals, but a handful carry most of the weight. In 2026
there are two audiences to satisfy at once: the classic ten blue links **and**
AI answers (AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini). Doing SEO well serves
both — Google itself frames "optimizing for AI search" as just good SEO.

## The pillars, in priority order

1. **Content quality, originality, search-intent match.** The dominant factor.
   The page must genuinely satisfy *why* someone searched — not just contain the
   keyword. Depth, accuracy, and originality beat volume. Thin or derivative
   content does not rank.
2. **E-E-A-T** (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust). Especially for
   "your money or your life" topics (health, finance, legal). Real authors, real
   credentials, primary sources, accurate claims.
3. **Authority / backlinks — earned only.** A few editorial links from relevant,
   high-authority sites outweigh hundreds of low-quality ones. You *earn* these
   with link-worthy content; you never build them with tools (see guardrails).
   Brand mentions and internal links also build authority.
4. **Technical health.** Crawlability, indexability, HTTPS, mobile-first parity,
   clean site architecture. A single `noindex` or `robots.txt` block can prevent
   ranking entirely. This is foundational, not optional.
5. **Page experience / Core Web Vitals.** LCP, INP, CLS. They improve UX and are
   a tie-breaker between close competitors; passing them does not by itself
   guarantee a ranking.
6. **Freshness & engagement.** For topics where recency matters, newer content
   wins. Update high-value pages regularly and show a real "last updated" date.
   Genuine user engagement (people finding what they came for) is a signal.

## What has faded — stop over-investing here

- Keyword density / stuffing (write naturally; cover the topic fully instead).
- Exact-match anchor-text link building (a spam signal now).
- Social signals as a direct ranking factor.
- Chasing word count for its own sake.

## The mental model

Think **relevance × authority × experience**. Relevance = you answer the intent.
Authority = trustworthy sites and users vouch for you. Experience = the page is
fast, clear, mobile-friendly, and easy to use. Weakness in any one caps the
others. Fix the biggest gap first.

## How to measure (honestly)

Use **Google Search Console** for impressions, clicks, average position, and
which queries you appear for — this is ground truth. Use PageSpeed Insights for
Core Web Vitals. Don't rely on scraped rank trackers (against Google's rules) or
vanity metrics. Never promise a specific position; set goals like "rank on page
1 for these 10 intent-matched queries within N months" and track progress.
