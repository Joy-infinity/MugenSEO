# Content & blog strategy

Content is the dominant ranking factor and the raw material AI engines cite. This
is where most durable ranking gains come from. The goal is fewer, deeper, genuinely
useful pieces that match real search intent — never mass-produced thin content.

## Search intent comes first

Before writing anything, classify the target query's intent and match it:

- **Informational** ("how to…", "what is…") → a clear, thorough guide/answer.
- **Commercial investigation** ("best…", "X vs Y", reviews) → honest comparisons,
  pros/cons, criteria.
- **Transactional** ("buy…", "pricing", "near me") → product/service/landing page
  that lets them act.
- **Navigational** (a brand/site) → make sure your own branded pages are clean.

Writing a great informational article for a transactional query (or vice-versa)
will not rank, no matter how good it is. Match the format searchers expect.

## Topic clusters & topical authority

Rank by owning a topic, not scattered keywords:

- Pick a core topic the site has real expertise in.
- Create a **pillar page** (broad, authoritative overview) plus **cluster pages**
  (each covering one sub-question in depth).
- **Internally link** clusters to the pillar and to each other with descriptive
  anchors. This signals topical authority and spreads link equity.

## Writing a strong SEO blog post (workflow)

1. **Pick the query + intent** from Search Console (queries you nearly rank for
   are the best targets) or keyword research; confirm intent by looking at what
   currently ranks.
2. **Research for depth**, including fresh angles via web search (Phase 3): recent
   news, new questions, data, examples. Bring something the ranking pages lack —
   original data, first-hand experience, a clearer explanation.
3. **Outline for extraction**: an H1 that states the topic; question-shaped H2s;
   a direct answer near the top; logical subsections; a short FAQ of real
   follow-up questions.
4. **Draft to genuinely help.** Specific, accurate, example-rich. Cite primary
   sources. Show real experience/credentials (E-E-A-T). Natural keyword use — no
   stuffing. If the user's own voice matters, write in it.
5. **On-page**: unique title (~50–60 chars) and meta description (~140–160),
   descriptive URL, alt text, internal links to/from related cluster pages,
   relevant Article/FAQ/HowTo schema (`templates/`).
6. **Add a real author** with bio/credentials, and a visible publish + "last
   updated" date.

## Freshness: the content refresh loop

Content decays. On a schedule (this ties into the 3-day loop):

- Find pages losing impressions/position in Search Console.
- Update stats, examples, screenshots, and dates; add newly-important subtopics;
  fix anything now inaccurate.
- Improve the intro's direct answer and the internal linking.
- Re-submit updated URLs for indexing.
  A refreshed, genuinely-improved page often beats writing a brand-new one.

## Quality bar (reject anything below it)

- Would this page be the **best, most useful result** a searcher could find?
- Does it show real experience or expertise, not just rephrased common knowledge?
- Is every claim accurate and, where it matters, sourced?
- Is it written for a person, not a crawler?

If no, improve it or don't publish it. Scaled thin content is a spam-policy risk
and won't rank anyway. AI is a fine tool for research, outlining and drafting —
but a human (and these quality checks) must own the result.
